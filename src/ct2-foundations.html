Learn CT-2 from scratch

Begin with what a computer does when you open a program. Work through one small example at a time until you can answer the CT-2 questions from Lectures 5–7.

You can begin without knowing C, binary, or assembly. Read one worked example, then try the question without opening the answer. Mark the lesson understood when you can explain the answer. Come back to it whenever you need to.

Start Lesson 1

Open the simulator lab to experiment with registers, memory, branches, stack frames, and instruction fields.

Pick up at the topic you need

  • Computer, registers and bits. Follow a program, then trace the first register writes.
  • Memory, branches and loops. Calculate a byte address, then trace a C condition and its assembly.
  • Functions and the stack. Track the result separately from the return address.
  • Machine code. Fill the fields and check the result by decoding it.
  • Mixed practice. Trace a program first, then solve its encoding questions.

Use the full reference lessons and visualisers for more examples once you know the steps. The final lesson points to specific past-paper questions.
