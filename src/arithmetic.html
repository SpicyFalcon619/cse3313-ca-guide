Restoring binary division

Divide ten by three in binary and see where each quotient bit comes from.

Start with ordinary long division

When you divide 10 by 3, you can fit three groups of 3 into 10. The answer 3 is the quotient, and the 1 left over is the remainder. Here the dividend is 10, the number being divided, and the divisor is 3, the size of each group.

In binary these inputs are 1010 and 0011. As in long division, read the dividend from the left. Bringing down one bit doubles the current remainder and adds that new bit. For example, bringing down a 1 after keeping a remainder of 2 gives 2 × 2 + 1 = 5. In binary that is 10 becoming 101.

Try the subtraction on every step

Subtract 3 from the partial remainder. If the subtraction is zero or positive, keep that result and write quotient bit 1. If it goes negative, the divisor was too large. Add 3 back to recover the partial remainder and write quotient bit 0. Recovering that value is the restoring step.

10 × 2 + 1 = 11 − 3 = −2Write 0. Restore 1.

12 × 2 + 1 = 55 − 3 = 2Write 1. Keep 2.
02 × 2 + 0 = 44 − 3 = 1Write 1. Keep 1.

Read the written bits in order: 0011, which is 3. The final remainder is 1. Check 3 × 3 + 1 = 10. A valid remainder is smaller than the divisor. An exact division leaves zero. A zero divisor is invalid.

Bring down 1, 1, 1, 0. The partial remainders before subtraction are 1, 3, 1, 2, in that order. Only the second subtraction succeeds, so the quotient bits are 0100. The last attempt restores 2. Check 3 × 4 + 2 = 14.
